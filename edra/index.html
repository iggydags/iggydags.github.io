    The whole opportunity, in one sentence

    The problem is narrower than “we need content”

    In September 2025, The Information categorized Edra as “another new AI consulting startup” using “consultants to make up for the agents’ shortcomings.” That framing is live, it is from a tier-one outlet, and it predates the launch. Every AI Strategist and Forward Deployed AI Engineer req posted since June makes it look more accurate to an outside reader, not less.

    Ninety days of good posts will not move that. A deliberate, argued, owned position will.

    The argument that beats it: Edra’s wedge is that documented process is a fiction, so you do not start from documentation. Distyl starts from SOPs the customer uploads. Palantir builds ontology by hand with humans in the room. Edra starts from exhaust: tickets, logs, emails, the record of what people actually did. That is a real architectural difference, it is defensible, and it is almost entirely unsaid in public.

    It is also the thing that separates “AI consultancy” from “software company with a deployment team,” which is the sentence the next round gets priced on.

    So the job in ninety days is not to fill a content calendar. It is to build the machine that makes the company incapable of going quiet again, ship the proof that already exists, and put the category argument on the record under the founders’ names.

    What this program is for

    Positioning first, because a calendar without one produces whatever is easiest to write rather than what the reader needs.

    Mission
    Make the operating knowledge of enterprises legible in public, starting with the deployments Edra has already run.
    Primary reader
    IT service and support operations leaders at companies above roughly 2,000 people. Directors and VPs of Service Delivery, Heads of IT Operations, CIO staff. Defining trait: they have already been burned. Edra’s own GTM lead has said prospects arrive with “a healthy scepticism” from “failed chatbot projects” and “RAG approaches that couldn’t deliver accurate results.” That is the most important fact about this reader. They are not looking to be excited. They are looking for a reason to believe, and their default is disbelief.
    Second reader
    Infrastructure and applied-AI engineers deciding where to work. Seventeen open reqs against about twenty people. This reader wants to know what the work actually is and whether the technical problem is real. Also the reader most repelled by marketing language.
    Third reader
    Press, analysts and investors deciding whether Edra is “an AI consulting startup” or “a software company with a deployment team.” Small audience, disproportionate consequence.
    Not for
    The general AI-curious reader. SMB and mid-market. Future-of-work commentary. Enormous audiences, near-zero overlap with the buyer, and writing for them is the most likely failure mode, because they respond fastest.
    The promise
    Every number labeled measured or projected. Every customer named. Every technical claim states what would falsify it. A reader who checks our sources finds them slightly more conservative than our copy, never less.

    We help enterprise operations leaders decide whether AI can actually run their processes by publishing evidence-first accounts of real deployments, including the parts that did not work, unlike the vendor blogs that publish projections as results and category essays with nothing underneath them.The distinction, in one sentence

    The distinction is not a subject. Everyone in this category writes about agents. The distinction is evidentiary standard, and it is available to Edra specifically because it has named customers and one bylined enterprise executive on record. Almost nobody at this stage does.

    Format owned long-formByline founderTarget week 4Length ~1,400 words

    Your process documentation is fiction

    Every company I deployed into at Palantir had a wiki. Most of them had a very good wiki. Someone had sat down, usually years earlier, usually under duress from an audit, and written how the work is supposed to happen. Then the person who wrote it left, the tooling changed twice, three exceptions became the rule, and the document kept sitting there being wrong in a confident, well-formatted way.

    This matters more than it used to. When the wiki was for onboarding, being wrong cost a new hire a week. Now people are pointing models at it. The instruction layer for an AI deployment is the same document nobody has trusted since 2019, and the model, having no way to know that, follows it perfectly.

    So the deployments fail, and the failure gets attributed to the model. It is almost never the model. Every enterprise runs two processes: the one in the wiki, and the one in the ticket queue. Only one of them is real, and it is not the one anybody wrote down.

    The second one is not hidden. It is in the tickets, the logs, the threads, the escalations, the four messages where somebody figured out the workaround and never told anyone. That record is complete, it is timestamped, and it is sitting in systems the company already pays for. The map nobody drew is already there. It just has to be read instead of asked for.

    Why this piece firstIt is the category argument in a form that does not mention a competitor, does not read as a launch post, and is true independent of whether Edra exists. Once documented process is fiction is on the record under a founder’s name, the counter-positioning piece and the customer stories become evidence for a claim readers have already met, instead of standalone marketing.

    What I cutThe product name, which does not appear until roughly word 900. A founder essay that mentions the product in the first paragraph is a press release wearing a byline.

    Format deployment record + 1-page PDFSource ASOS plc newsroom, publicTarget weeks 3–4Length ~900 words

    ASOS had 400 knowledge base articles. Half of them were wrong.

    Not wrong in a way anyone had noticed. Wrong in the ordinary way documentation goes wrong: written correctly, three tooling changes ago, by people who have since moved on.

    ASOS came to us with 20,000 historical IT service tickets and 400 knowledge base articles, and a question their EVP of Technology has since described publicly in his own words: what does it actually take to make a knowledge base an AI system can stand on?

    We read the tickets, not the articles. Edra reverse-engineers process from the record of the work rather than the description of it, which meant the first output was not a recommendation. It was a diff. Roughly half of the existing articles had drifted from what the tickets showed people actually doing. And 200 topics were being resolved regularly by humans and had never been documented at all, the workaround that lives in one person’s head, repeated forty times a year, invisible to every system.

    Ticket coverage went from about 30 percent to about 90 percent. On ASOS’s own assessment, roughly 30 percent of inbound questions can now be resolved automatically, with a further 30 percent ready for automation once system-level actions are enabled. That is measured capacity, and we are going to keep saying it that way. The deflection number will be worth reporting when it is a number and not a projection.

    Why lead with the failure“Half your documentation is wrong” is a sentence every reader in this buyer seat recognizes about their own company, which earns the next paragraph. The alternative opening, some version of ASOS partnered with Edra to transform their knowledge foundation, is true and gets skimmed.

    The hedge stays inParagraph five deliberately gives back the stronger headline. A CIO who checks the ASOS source and finds we inflated it stops reading everything else we publish. Precision is the cheapest credibility available to a Series A company.

    Last year a reporter described us as an AI consulting startup that uses consultants to cover for what the agents cannot do.

    I have not answered that publicly. I want to, because the honest answer is better than the silence.

    We do forward-deploy. That is not a hedge against the product, it is how the product gets built. We spent years at Palantir doing the manual version of this: sitting with an operations team, learning how the work actually happens, and hand-encoding it. It worked and it did not scale, because the encoding was a person.

    So the question is not whether humans are in the loop. It is what they are doing, and whether that shrinks.

    Six weeks of an engineer reading tickets to map a support process is now about four days of a customer reviewing what the system already drafted. The human moved from author to editor. That is the whole company. If that number stops falling, the thesis is wrong, and we will say so.

    Happy to be held to it.

This version concedes the accurate part, reframes the disputed part as a measurable claim, and volunteers a falsification condition, which is the only move that makes an outside reader believe the rest.

    What I would measure, and what I would not do

    Impressions are not a metric. The failure mode for this role is scope sprawl into things that feel like marketing and produce nothing.

    Leading

    • Artifacts shipped per month against a target of five to six
    • Consecutive weeks without a gap. The posting says do not let the company go quiet, so this is the literal KPI
    • Founder pieces published per month, target two
    • Customer stories moved from unreachable to readable
    • Founder time actually spent, tracked honestly, because it is the binding constraint

    Lagging

    • Inbound demo requests attributed to content, tracked from day one
    • Sales cycle: does the ASOS record get used, and do AEs say it changes calls
    • Recruiting: candidates who cite a specific piece, and drop-off after they read the JD
    • Category language adoption: are analysts, competitors or customers using “process exhaust”
    • Press: coverage after March 2026 is currently zero. Any non-launch, non-funding coverage is a step change

    On search, specifically

    I would not build this program around search in the first ninety days, and I want to be direct about why. The buyer arrives through referral, analyst conversation, peer recommendation or a founder’s post. The category has no settled search language yet: “process exhaust” has effectively no volume, and the terms that do have volume are contested by vendors with years of domain authority and budgets Edra does not have. Ranking there is a two-year project. More to the point, there is no corpus to optimise. SEO is a structuring discipline and there is nothing yet to structure.

    So: months one to three, technical hygiene only, a few hours of work that prevents debt which is expensive to unwind. Months four to six, real keyword research once there are twelve to eighteen pieces and actual query data. Months six to twelve, hubs around the pillars that earned one. The trap I am avoiding is choosing pillars for search volume rather than for the reader, which in this category produces “What is agentic AI?” posts that rank for nobody who buys.

    Not in the first ninety days

    • No rebrand. The site is fine. The problem is what it does not surface.
    • No paid, no demand gen, no ABM tooling. There is no attribution stack and no team to run it.
    • No PR agency. With this founder story and these customers, earned coverage comes from the writing. An agency at this stage produces meetings.
    • No stories with unnamed customers. “A leading retailer” is worth nothing. Named or not shipped.
    • No volume for its own sake. The recovery is not thirty mediocre posts. It is six pieces good enough that people send them to each other.

    The proof is already public.
