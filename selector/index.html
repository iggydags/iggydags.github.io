Operating Principle

Stabilize before automating, automate before hiring. Every recurring loop gets one of three treatments: automated (if the pattern is stable), documented as an SOP (if it still needs judgment), or killed (if nobody misses it). Humans keep the work that requires judgment and relationships; everything else goes on the backlog.

Days 1–30: Stabilize — make every open loop visible

  • Inventory all open loops across Slack, Gmail, Calendar, docs, and meeting notes: client commitments, SOW follow-ups, pricing questions, finance items, tooling access. One tracker, one owner per item, one review ritual.
  • Stand up the weekly operating rhythm: a Monday review of client status, blocked work, and finance/admin items; agendas prepared before every meeting, decisions and next steps captured after.
  • Sweep the finance hygiene backlog: receipts, invoices, bills, and approvals reconciled to current, with a simple capture habit so the pile never rebuilds.
  • Deliverable at day 30: zero stale follow-ups older than one week, and a prioritized automation backlog reviewed with the founders.

Days 31–60: Systemize — automate the top frictions

Candidate backlog, sequenced by frequency times pain, cheapest wins first. Each item names the trigger, the tool, and what stays human:

FrictionAutomation approachStays human
Meeting notes → action itemsAI extraction from transcripts/notes into the tracker, auto-assigned with due datesJudgment on priority and owner
Customer schedulingScheduling links plus an AI triage layer for multi-party threads; focus time protected by defaultHigh-stakes or sensitive scheduling
Client status updatesWeekly draft auto-assembled from tracker state; I edit and sendThe voice, and anything with news in it
SOW / proposal follow-upPipeline tracker with aging alerts; drafts pre-generated for routine nudgesNegotiation and pricing calls
Receipts, invoices, billsForwarding rules plus AI extraction into a ledger the accountant works fromApprovals and exceptions
Research requestsStanding AI research workflow: brief in, sourced memo draft outThe synthesis and the recommendation

Deliverable at day 60: the top three frictions running as AI-assisted workflows in production, each documented well enough that anyone can operate or improve it.

Days 61–90: Compound — make improvement the default

  • Weekly automation review as a standing ritual: what broke, what got manual again, what earned its way onto the backlog.
  • Simple metrics on the wall: stale follow-ups, median time-to-response on client items, share of recurring workflows automated, hours of coordination recovered per week.
  • Documentation as a byproduct, not a project: every workflow ships with its SOP, so the operating system survives me being on vacation.
  • Deliverable at day 90: a functioning improvement engine, and a founder team spending measurably more time on clients and product, and less on coordination.
