Part 1 · How I would approach this

Four questions, answered in order. About half a page.

1

First day or two: what I would ask, and of whom

Numbers, not status. Supply Chain first: how many devices need the component swap, how many parts are on hand, the lead time on the rest. That number sets the calendar. Engineering: which devices take v3 with nobody involved, which need a remote assist, which need a visit; whether a fleet table with firmware and hardware revision exists; who can pull a rollback without a meeting. Field Ops: technician count and real visits per day. Customer Success: fragile accounts and pilot volunteers. Sales: renewals closing inside the window. Marketing: what the announcement depends on. Finance: the envelope and the variance threshold. Leadership: does “rolled out” mean the device runs v3, or the customer uses the alerting? Two different end dates. Better to know on day two than in week six.

2

Phases, and why in that order

1,400 devices is not one rollout. It is four, sorted by who has to touch the device, and the phases fall out of that. Long-lead item first regardless of when it is needed. Announcement last regardless of how ready Marketing is. Ascending risk in between.

Week 1Segment and unblockCohort table. Parts ordered on an estimate with buffer. Rollback criteria locked. Renewals cross-checked.
Week 2Pilot~24 devices, friendly sites. Proves it survives live service. Hard gate.
Weeks 3–5Remote wavesBulk of the fleet, lowest risk first. Hold point between waves.
Weeks 5–7Field and swapVisits batched by geography. Swap sites land here because week 1 unblocked them.
Week 8CloseTail, cost true-up, retro, then the announcement. Announcing early manufactures tickets.
3

Biggest risks and dependencies, and how I would get ahead of them

Swap parts are the one hard dependency. Field Ops cannot start those sites without them.Order in week 1 on an estimate. If lead time slips, swap sites become their own late wave rather than blocking all field work.
Field capacity is fixed. You cannot hire technicians mid-rollout.Run visit-days required against available in week 1. If it fails, leadership decides in week 1, not week 6.
The customer is the risk nobody puts on the plan. An update that fails at eight on a Friday is a churn event.Off-hours windows. Rollback authority on the on-call engineer. Support staffed the morning after every wave. No visit and renewal call in the same week.
4

What needs cross-department coordination, and what a single team just runs

Coordinate if it has a handoff, a shared date, or a shared customer touch. Supply Chain shipping before Field Ops installs is a handoff. Marketing’s announcement against rollout coverage is a shared date. A customer who gets a notice, a visit and a renewal call in the same week is a shared touch. Everything else is one line on the tracker with an owner and a date; how Engineering runs QA or Field Ops routes technicians is not the project’s business. The way this role fails is coordinating things that did not need it. So the cadence stays small: a 20-minute Monday sync on blocked rows only, a written Friday update off the tracker, escalation only when a decision is required.

Part 3 · Week-two update to leadership

Posted to the leadership channel, Friday 25 September. Every number is read off the tracker.

BarSys OS v3 rollout · end of week 2Status on plan, one decision needed

Pilot cleared, two days ahead of the gate. All 24 devices across six locations are on v3, with one rollback on a calibration fault that Engineering has since automated out. Three support tickets, all on alert setup, none on pour accuracy, and Customer Success has already rewritten the setup guidance, which is what a pilot is for. Marketing’s sales battlecard also landed a week early, so Sales is briefed ahead of the 11 renewals in the window. Wave 1 opens Monday, 452 devices.

One thing is genuinely at risk. The flow-meter modules for the 58 oldest devices came back at a five-week lead time rather than two, putting parts on site 21 October and leaving eight working days to cover 14 locations. Cohort D, about 4% of the fleet, will likely run past 6 November.

You will have a one-page decision from me Monday with both options costed: an alternate supplier at roughly 15% more, or a two-week tail. My recommendation is the supplier; a late tail costs more in customer calls than the parts do. I would rather you choose now than in week six.

Cost: $8,400 committed, $28,000 forecast against the $46,000 envelope. Everything else is on track. Live detail is in the tracker.
