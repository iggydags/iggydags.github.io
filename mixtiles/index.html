Moving isn't a demographic. It's a deadline. Tens of millions of Americans move each year, and for roughly ninety days each one becomes a different kind of consumer: high intent, time-boxed, and emotionally primed to answer the exact question your whole portfolio answers — how do I make this place feel like mine? The catch: the moment is invisible until it's over. Nobody announces a move to Mixtiles; they announce it to their landlord, their agent, their mover, and the postal service. So the company you build around this moment is less a new product than a new distribution layer: own the channels that know someone is moving before their walls do.

Four opportunities, in the order I'd test them

Click each one. Ranked by how cheaply we can learn whether it's real.

Real estate agents buy closing gifts at scale, they're chronically unimaginative about it, and it's a marketing expense they already budget. A "first photo in your new home" Mixtiles gift, sent by the agent, beats a candle and doubles as a lead-gen machine: the agent pays the CAC, the gift arrives exactly at T+0, and every tile on a wall recruits the housewarming guests. I worked in residential real estate data at Compass — agents adopt anything that makes them memorable at closing.

Moving is one of the last gift-heavy life moments with no registry norm. Build one: a mover shares a link, friends fund tiles, plants, and Oasis pieces for the new place. It converts one mover into ten gift-buying customers and creates the social announcement channel the brand currently lacks.

Movers, utilities-setup platforms, renters-insurance apps, and building welcome flows all touch the mover at T–30 with permissioned attention. Test paid placement and welcome-kit bundles; measure CAC against the existing blended rate and kill anything that doesn't beat it.

A "new home starter" SKU designed for the moment: a layout kit for the first bare wall, sized to the photos people actually have from the move — the sold sign, the keys, the first-night pizza on the floor. Merchandising, not invention.

What I'd test first, and how I'd know

Start with the closing-gift channel: it needs no consumer discovery, no new product, and one salesperson — me. Pilot with 50 agents across two metros, an agent portal that could start as a Typeform, and gift volume as the input metric. Three numbers decide everything:

Agent repeat rate
Do they gift again on the next closing?
Recipient conversion
Does the gifted household buy within 90 days?
Referral attach
Do housewarming guests convert?

If recipient conversion beats the blended new-customer rate, the channel scales with the agent population — and the registry becomes the natural second act. Sixty days, one owner, real numbers.
