A First 90 Days

Built from the posting's own success criteria. Every assumption gets tested against reality in week one, and the plan gets revised accordingly.

Operating principle. The CBO's time is the scarcest commercial asset in the building. Every cadence I run either protects it or wastes it, so each one earns its slot: right attendees, tight agenda, documented output, and an action item that actually closes. I ran exactly this rhythm for a CEO across two venues for two years, and I ran the bars themselves before that, so the beverage world Barsys sells into is home turf, not homework.
Day 1 Shadowing every cadence, writing everything down
Day 1Day 30Day 60Day 90
  • Shadow every cadence on the CBO's calendar before changing anything: who attends, what it decides, what it produces, and where its outputs currently go to die.
  • Stand up the single live action tracker spanning every cadence and initiative: one owner and one deadline per item, aging visible, chased proactively, closed only when confirmed done.
  • Tighten each cadence's format with the CBO: agenda structure set, pre-reads out the day before, notes distilled to decisions and next steps and routed within the hour.
Day 30, in the posting's own words: "Every cadence the CBO owns has a tight agenda, the right attendees, and a clear, documented output. No action item falls through the cracks."
  • Build the lightweight analysis layer under each cadence: partnership pipeline status, revenue-channel performance, and competitive context, sanity-checked before anything reaches leadership.
  • Turn cadence outputs into the recurring business update the CBO can share up and out, drafted automatically from tracker state, edited by me, sent on a schedule.
  • Start pattern-spotting across cadences: recurring blockers, slipping owners, and process gaps flagged with a proposed fix attached, not just an observation.
Day 60. Every cadence connects to a number, the CBO's recurring update writes itself from the tracker, and prep time per meeting has visibly dropped.
  • Support board-update and leadership-review materials: translate a quarter of cadence outputs into a clear narrative of commercial progress.
  • Automate the recurring mechanics with AI tooling where the pattern is stable (note capture, tracker updates, status drafts) so my hours shift from documenting to analyzing.
  • Bring a sequencing point of view to the table: which commercial priorities are compounding, which are stalled, and what I'd propose we do about it.
Day 90. The operating rhythm runs without heroics, and I arrive at the first-year bar early: a trusted voice in how priorities get sequenced, with a bid on the table for an owned workstream.
