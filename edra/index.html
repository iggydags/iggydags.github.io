02

The problem is narrower than “we need content”

In September 2025, The Information categorized Edra as “another new AI consulting startup” using “consultants to make up for the agents’ shortcomings.” That framing is live, it is from a tier-one outlet, and it predates the launch. Every AI Strategist and Forward Deployed AI Engineer req posted since June makes it look more accurate to an outside reader, not less.

Ninety days of good posts will not move that. A deliberate, argued, owned position will.

The argument that beats it: Edra’s wedge is that documented process is a fiction, so you do not start from documentation. Distyl starts from SOPs the customer uploads. Palantir builds ontology by hand with humans in the room. Edra starts from exhaust: tickets, logs, emails, the record of what people actually did. That is a real architectural difference, it is defensible, and it is almost entirely unsaid in public.

It is also the thing that separates “AI consultancy” from “software company with a deployment team,” which is the sentence the next round gets priced on.

So the job in ninety days is not to fill a content calendar. It is to build the machine that makes the company incapable of going quiet again, ship the proof that already exists, and put the category argument on the record under the founders’ names.

04

The ninety days

Three phases. Each ends on a scorecard I would accept being judged against.

Days 1–30 · Stop the bleeding, build the spine

Edra owns its own publishing surface, has shipped its first real case study, and has a weekly cadence that does not depend on anyone’s mood.

Week 1 · Intake, no output

The temptation is to post something in the first week to show motion. I would not. Week one buys the raw material that makes the next twelve weeks fast.

  • Read every customer deployment doc, every FDE engagement log, every pilot readout. Not the marketing summaries, the actual working artifacts.
  • Sit in on two live customer calls. Take notes on the language customers use, not the language we use.
  • 45-minute recorded interview with Eugen: why he left Palantir, what he believes about enterprise context that the market does not, what Edra is that nobody has said out loud yet.
  • 45-minute recorded interview with Yannis: the technical thesis, why rule-learning is tractable now and was not in 1985, where he thinks it breaks.
  • 30 minutes each with Filippos, the FDE team, and whoever runs the ASOS account.
  • Audit every public artifact Edra has produced. Two hours. It is a short list.
Output: one document. A voice-and-position brief covering what we claim, what we can prove, what we cannot yet prove, the terms we own versus the ones we borrow, and the three sentences that separate us from Distyl, Palantir and Glean. It goes to the founders for correction, not approval. The corrections are the point.

Week 2 · The surface

  • Ship edra.ai/blog. The site is Framer. This is days of work, not weeks. If it stalls on a dependency it goes up as a subdomain and gets merged later. Having no owned publishing surface five months post-launch is the most fixable problem on the list.
  • Migrate the launch post off LinkedIn Pulse onto the owned domain, canonical, with the Pulse version pointing back.
  • Republish Filippos’s expert-systems essay as a proper post, edited, with a real title and a real argument. It already exists. It needs a home.
  • Establish the weekly hold: Monday 30-minute recorded founder session, Wednesday ship, Friday two-line internal note on what went out and what it did.

Weeks 3–4 · First proof, first position

  • Ship the ASOS case study. The highest-leverage single artifact available. Their EVP already published his own version, which means internal approval is largely pre-cleared and reference risk is low. Two versions: a long owned piece on edra.ai, and a tight one-page PDF the AEs can attach to an email.
One thing I would insist on. The automation number gets stated the way ASOS states it. “Can now be resolved automatically” and “ready for automation once system-level actions are enabled” is assessed capacity, not measured deflection. Our launch post said “projected.” Later copy dropped the hedge. That hedge goes back in. It costs a marginally weaker headline and buys a proof point that survives contact with a skeptical CIO, which is the only kind worth having.
  • Ship Eugen’s first long-form piece under his own name. Working title: Your process documentation is fiction. The argument: every enterprise has two processes, the one in the wiki and the one in the ticket queue, and every AI deployment that starts from the first one fails. This is the category argument, it is true, it is provable with our own customer data, and it is the sentence that reframes us from consultancy to software.
  • Restart the company channels on a fixed rhythm behind those two artifacts. Not filler. Distribution for things that exist.
Day 30 scorecard

Blog live on the owned domain. Three published pieces: Filippos edited, ASOS case study, Eugen essay. Weekly cadence running four consecutive weeks. Voice brief in circulation. Second and third case studies in customer legal review.

Days 31–60 · Cadence, category, recruiting

The cadence is boring and reliable. Both founders have a public voice. The category argument is on the record in more than one place.

Category work

  • Ship the counter-positioning piece. Not a competitor takedown, an architectural argument: the three ways to teach a model your business, which are upload your SOPs, hand-build an ontology, or derive it from exhaust. What each costs, where each breaks. Distyl and Palantir named, fairly. This is the piece other people quote, which is the only real distribution.
  • Address the consulting framing directly, under Eugen’s name. Do not dodge it. The honest version is stronger than the evasion: yes we forward-deploy, here is exactly what the humans do, here is the work that used to take an FDE six weeks and now takes the product four days, here is how we know it is compressing. Owning it beats letting one sentence in The Information stand as the only public answer. This is the piece I would push hardest for and expect the most resistance on.
  • Get Yannis on the record. He is the technically credible half, he has no public presence, and he is in London. Two formats that work async: a written technical essay on continual learning and the maintenance-cost question, and one podcast. Not a big one. A good one, where the host understands the problem.

Recruiting narrative

Seventeen open reqs against about twenty people. The engineering brand is currently doing zero work.

  • An engineering philosophy piece on what a Forward Deployed AI Engineer at Edra actually does day to day, concrete enough that the right person self-selects in and the wrong person self-selects out.
  • Rewrite the job descriptions. The AI Strategist req currently protests that it is “not account management, project management, or consulting,” which reads defensively. Say what it is instead.
  • A Hacker News presence that is not a hiring post with zero replies. The expert-systems material is genuinely HN-shaped. That is how you reach people who will never see a LinkedIn ad.
Day 60 scorecard

Eight consecutive weeks of cadence. Both founders published under their own names. Counter-positioning piece live. Second case study shipped. Recruiting content live against the open reqs. First inbound attributable to content.

Days 61–90 · Compounding

The function is a system, not a person. A quarter of forward inventory, honest measurement, and category language being used by people who do not work here.

  • Third case study ships (Cushman & Wakefield or Marosa). Three real, named, numbered customer stories on a page that currently has zero is the deliverable I would most want to be judged on.
  • Sales enablement, properly. One-pagers per use case across ITSM, technical support and compliance. An objection-handling document built from real objections the AEs are hearing. A rebuttal to “how is this different from Distyl” an AE can deliver in ninety seconds. GTM is the department this role reports into. It should be able to point at revenue support, not just publishing.
  • Founder voice reaches the second tier. Podcasts, a conference talk, one earned byline. By day 90 an inbound request for Eugen should have somewhere to point.
  • Build the content system: a running inventory of at least a quarter of ideas sourced from customer calls and engineering work, a repeatable case-study intake so the next one takes two weeks instead of six, and a written voice guide so a second hire does not restart from zero.
  • The 90-day readout. Honest. What worked, what did not, what I was wrong about, what the next ninety need. Including the parts that make me look bad. That is the only version worth writing.

What I would measure

Impressions are not a metric.

Leading

  • Artifacts shipped per month against target of five to six
  • Consecutive weeks without a gap in cadence. The posting says do not let the company go quiet, so this is the literal KPI
  • Founder pieces published per month, target two, one each
  • Case studies moved from zero to live
  • Founder time actually spent, tracked honestly, because it is the binding constraint

Lagging

  • Inbound demo requests attributed to content, tracked from day one
  • Sales cycle: does the ASOS study get used, and do AEs say it changes calls
  • Recruiting: candidates who cite a specific piece, and drop-off after they read the JD
  • Category language adoption: are analysts, competitors or customers using “process exhaust” and “documented versus actual process”
  • Press: coverage after March 2026 is currently zero. Any non-launch, non-funding earned coverage is a step change
07

What I would not do

Naming these because the failure mode for this role is scope sprawl into things that feel like marketing and produce nothing.

  • No rebrand. The site is fine. The problem is that there is nothing on it.
  • No paid, no demand gen, no ABM tooling in the first ninety days. There is no attribution stack and no team to run it.
  • No PR agency. With this founder story and these logos, earned coverage comes from the writing. An agency at this stage produces meetings.
  • No case studies with unnamed customers. “A leading retailer” is worth nothing. Named or not shipped.
  • No volume for its own sake. The company has been quiet for three and a half months. The recovery is not thirty mediocre posts. It is six pieces good enough that people send them to each other.
08

What I would need

Stated as conditions, not requests, because the plan does not work without them.

01A recurring calendar hold with each founder. 45 minutes weekly with Eugen, 30 with Yannis, recorded. This is the single point of failure. Founder-led content is determined by founder appetite, not writer skill.
02Direct customer access. Introductions to the ASOS, HubSpot and Cushman & Wakefield contacts in week one. Enterprise legal review is the long pole on every case study. Start in week three and it lands by day 30. Start in week six and it does not land at all.
03Publish authority. I ship on the cadence without a founder approval gate on every piece. Founder-bylined work gets founder sign-off. Everything else goes when it is ready. A review queue is how cadence dies.
04Engineering time, roughly two hours a month, from Filippos and the FDE team. The technical material is the most differentiated asset in the building and it does not come from the founders.
05A decision on the automation-number hedge. I want measured claims and projected claims labeled differently in all public material. This is a small argument now and a large one later.
06Clarity on scope beyond ninety days. Whether this becomes a function with headcount or stays a role of one changes what I build. I would rather have the honest answer than the encouraging one.
09

The risk I would name out loud

The part that argues against hiring me

The plan above assumes the founders want to be public. Nothing in the record suggests they do. Two posts in two years from Eugen, no X account for Yannis, no essays, no podcasts, no talks. It is possible the quiet since launch is not a resourcing gap but a revealed preference.

If that is the case, this role produces good work that nobody with authority stands behind, and the company stays quiet with a more expensive headcount. That is the documented failure mode for founding content hires, and it is not fixed by hiring harder.

So the honest version of this plan is that weeks one and two are as much a test of founder appetite as they are onboarding. If the recorded sessions happen on schedule and the corrections come back sharp, everything above is achievable and probably conservative. If the first three sessions get moved, the plan changes and I would say so in week three rather than week thirty.

I would rather start that conversation now than discover it at day 90.

The proof is already public. I would like to be the one who assembles it.
