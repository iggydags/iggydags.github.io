Operating principle. Stabilize before automating, automate before hiring. Every recurring loop gets one of three treatments: automated if the pattern is stable, documented as an SOP if it still needs judgment, or killed if nobody misses it. Humans keep the work that requires judgment and relationships. Everything else goes on the backlog.
Day 1 Inventorying every open loop
Day 1Day 30Day 60Day 90
  • Inventory all open loops across Slack, Gmail, Calendar, docs, and meeting notes: client commitments, SOW follow-ups, pricing questions, finance items, tooling access. One tracker, one owner per item, one review ritual.
  • Stand up the weekly operating rhythm: a Monday review of client status, blocked work, and finance/admin items. Agendas prepared before every meeting, decisions and next steps captured after.
  • Sweep the finance hygiene backlog: receipts, invoices, bills, and approvals reconciled to current, with a simple capture habit so the pile never rebuilds.
Day 30 deliverable. Zero stale follow-ups older than one week, and a prioritized automation backlog reviewed with the founders.

Work the backlog below, sequenced by frequency times pain, cheapest wins first. Each item names the automation approach and, just as deliberately, what stays human.

Day 60 deliverable. The top three frictions running as AI-assisted workflows in production, each documented well enough that anyone can operate or improve it.
  • Weekly automation review as a standing ritual: what broke, what got manual again, what earned its way onto the backlog.
  • Simple metrics on the wall: stale follow-ups, median time-to-response on client items, share of recurring workflows automated, hours of coordination recovered per week.
  • Documentation as a byproduct, not a project: every workflow ships with its SOP, so the operating system survives me being on vacation.
Day 90 deliverable. A functioning improvement engine, and a founder team spending measurably more time on clients and product, and less on coordination.
