The benchmark math. Deductions run 1–5% of gross revenue on Confido's own published numbers, up to 5–20% with trade included, and 30–60% of them are invalid. Most brands never finish the sort.
Windows expire first. UNFI and KeHE allow roughly 30 days to dispute; many retailers 60–90. Every month of manual triage converts recoverable dollars into lost ones.
Why this is the wedge. The output is a dollar figure a founder forwards to their CFO. Gate the PDF export with an email and this is the inbound engine, running while everyone sleeps.

It teaches the buyer's P&L back to them. The most-shared asset in CPG is always some version of “why your $5.99 SKU nets 14¢.” Founders screenshot this and argue about it. That's distribution.
Every bar is a product line. Trade spend is TPM. Deductions are deduction management. The chart doubles as a product tour without saying so.
Formats. Embeddable widget, PDF one-pager per vertical, and the 60-second vertical video walking one SKU down the waterfall.

The asymmetry is the lesson. Under-forecasting burns margin you never get back; over-forecasting ties up cash and spoils inventory. Skew the slider and watch which side of the P&L pays.
The second product's opener. Confido forecasts $1B+ in retail sales. This meter is how that capability introduces itself to a brand still forecasting in a spreadsheet.
Accuracy prices itself. The gap between the marker and ten points better is the number that justifies the demo. No claims required: the buyer set every input.

Each tool ships in three formats: embeddable calculator, gated PDF export, and a 60-second vertical. Benchmark defaults from Confido's published blog (1–5% of gross revenue; 30–60% invalid) and industry guides (5–20% with trade included). Illustrative teaching models, not financial advice.

confido.io currently 302-redirects to an offshore gambling site. Anyone who types the obvious domain lands there. Cheap to fix relative to the brand risk; I'd put it on the week-one list.

What's already working: the blog ranks on page one for high-intent queries like “retailer deductions,” the 200-brand logo wall is real social proof, the nav is cleanly organized by buyer team, and SOC 2 is visible. The gaps below are conversion and compounding, not credibility.

P1
No lead magnet, no email captureThe homepage's only door is “Book a Demo.” Every visitor not ready to buy—most of them—bounces unrecorded.
ShipThe leakage calculator on-domain, free to run, PDF gated by email. Homepage and every post get capture. This page is the working prototype.
P2
200+ customers, 3 case studiesThe highest-leverage content backlog in CPG SaaS, sitting unpublished. Rebel Creamery's “$100K+ recovered” is the template.
ShipA case-study engine: numbers-first template, one per week, tagged by vertical and retailer so sales can send the exact-match story.
P2
No comparison pagesBuyers searching “deduction management software” and “[competitor] alternative” land on competitors' pages making the comparison for them.
ShipAn honest comparison hub: vs. spreadsheets, vs. point tools, vs. legacy TPM suites. Honest wins these pages; they convert at bottom-of-funnel rates.
P3
The unowned search surface: deduction codesAR teams google “Walmart deduction code 22,” “Kroger deduction codes,” “UNFI MCB meaning” weekly, and land on forums and PDFs.
ShipA programmatic code library: 50 pages at launch, one per code family, each ending in the calculator. Compounds for years.
P3
Newsletter equity accrues off-domainThe newsletter lives on a beehiiv subdomain: subscribers, archives, and SEO value all building someone else's asset.
ShipOn-domain archive and capture; beehiiv can stay as the send engine. The content is good, the plumbing leaks.
P4
Six vertical pages, one templateThe industry pages exist but read interchangeably. Refrigerated and frozen brands have the worst deduction pain and the least tailored page.
ShipOne killer vertical page first (cold chain), with vertical-specific benchmarks, case study, and calculator defaults. Then clone the pattern.
